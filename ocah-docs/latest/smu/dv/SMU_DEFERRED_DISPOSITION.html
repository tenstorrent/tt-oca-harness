SMU deferred and OUT disposition

This chapter has two parts. The table below is the catalog of every SMU test name classified deferred or OUT, and none of those names is in the hosted credit set hosted; Section 1 is the record of every stand-in, force, skip and waiver on a proof path of an enrolled test. The table first. Three kinds of name are in it. Fifteen have a test body and are enrolled in testlists/all.toml; their bucket says which group holds them and why that group is not credit. Fifteen are enrolled in no testlist, because this bench cannot run them or they cannot pass on it; their bucket says what each needs. A deferred body, and its sequence where it had one, is kept in git history rather than on disk and is restored when the blocker named in its row clears. The rest never had a test body here. None of the last two kinds is reportable as PASS. Enrolled names outside hosted that carry neither classification — the lifecycle, debug-gating, chain and elaboration leaves — are not in this table: each is carded in SMU_VPLAN.adoc and enrolled in a named group of testlists/all.toml.

Buckets:

  • SEP1-enrolled — has a body and runs in the wrapper all group with real SEP instruction retirement (sep_real_fw, sep_entropy, or fw_boot for the SEP smoke). Not hosted credit, and no CI workflow schedules those groups.

  • SEP1-blocked — written against the wrapper bench, enrolled in no testlist: the SEP-driven SMC bring-up polls SMC SRAM that no sys-inbound master can reach, so the leaf cannot pass by construction. Re-enrols in all the day the SMC SRAM path exists.

  • not-runnable — has a body in git history, enrolled in no testlist: the claim it checks is not met by the RTL as built, the bench cannot produce the condition it needs, or it needs the smu #(.CFG(smu_pkg::NoSepCfg)) elaboration, which this package does not build. The row names the condition.

  • OUT-until-SEP1 — no body; a scenario driven by SEP firmware. The bench elaborates the real SEP and smu_sep_smoke_test retires its instructions, so a leaf written for one of these names runs SEP firmware, needs the RISC-V toolchain for its c_build stage, and joins the groups outside hosted (fw_boot, sep_real_fw) that no workflow schedules.

  • child-TB-owned — protocol or CPU depth belongs to the SMC, SEP, or DTP TB.

  • waived — superseded, toggle-as-pass, stimulus-pin gap, or leftover catalog name.

Enrolled names outside this catalog:

  • The JTAG2AXI / OTP / filter set in hosted is recorded in the Component Signoff section of hw/sys/smu/dv/docs/SMU_VPLAN.adoc, which also records ATOP reject as OUT.

  • smu_ext_boot_seq_gate_test and smu_smc_mailbox_int_test are enrolled in hosted (testlists/all.toml).

  • smu_ext_axi_global_addr_smoke_test is in the table below under not-runnable.

1. Bench stand-ins and exceptions

A stand-in is anything that answers in place of the mechanism a claim is about: a testbench force, a simulation-only path selected by a plusarg, a configuration waiver, a loopback where a chain would be, a compiled-in substitute for a product cell, or a model inside the product composition. Nine sit on proof paths of enrolled SMU tests. Each has one entry of six fields. The first three — the claim that is accepted with the stand-in in place, why the real path cannot be used, and the exact scope — are filled from the tree. The last three — approver, approval date against a revision, and review or expiry date — are filled by a person; until they are, the entry is a declaration and clears nothing, and every test on its scope is graded as if the stand-in were undeclared. pending in those fields means exactly that. Each entry describes the tree at this branch’s head, not an older revision: the stand-ins and the entries that declare them move in the same commits, so a hash written into this file is stale the moment the file is edited. What makes an entry checkable instead is the command in Section 1.1 — one per entry, each naming a string a reader can see for themselves. An approver fills the Date, revision field with the revision they checked and the command they ran.

1.6. +define+ASSERTS_OFF

Table 8. <code>+define+ASSERTS_OFF</code>

Claim accepted

No SystemVerilog assertion inside the DUT, the vendored common cells or the testbench is a checker on any run this package records. Every enrolled verdict is a Python compare in a sequence or scoreboard, or the firmware’s own terminal loop, and every card in SMU_VPLAN.adoc offers such a compare as its evidence; no card offers an SVA. The vendored pulp-platform/common_cells assertions are guarded by COMMON_CELLS_ASSERTS_OFF, which hw/common/dv/configs/profiles/native.toml sets for every DUT, and the OCAH ASSERTS_OFF guard is set on every Verilator target of smu_sim_cfg.toml so that the immediate assertions in the PeakRDL register blocks and the SEP blocks do not fire on the two-state, X-free elaboration for reasons unrelated to any enrolled claim. Turning them on needs a per-assertion review of which fire legitimately on Verilator, which is the shared shim and assertion-gating work of issue #1164.

1.7. Scan-chain loopbacks

Table 9. Scan-chain loopbacks

Claim accepted

A bit shifted out of any of the DTP’s five host scan ports returns on its scan input one TCK later, so smu_dtp_bsr_extest_loopback_test proves the DTP’s EXTEST decode, its scan-control gating and the IEEE 1149.1 TDO retimer against a zero-length testbench BSR chain, and smu_dtp_scan_chain_boundary_test proves the iJTAG SIB cascade, the DFT/DFD/STAP host-chain selection and the STAP TDO enable and TMS behaviour against the other four zero-length loops. Neither proves a boundary-scan register, pad cells, or an adopter scan chain.

Why the real path cannot be used

The wrapper has no boundary-scan cells and no adopter DFD or DFT scan chains; those host ports terminate at pads the open-source tree does not model.

Scope

tb/tb_wrapper_top.sv loops all five host scan chains on their own nets, each *_host_scan_out_o driving its *_host_scan_in_i (bsr_scan_loop, stap_scan_loop, dfd_scan_loop, dft_sec_scan_loop, dft_scan_loop), and returns the I/O and extra STAP host TDO to the host TDI (stap_io_tdo_w; stap_extra_tdi_w[0] = stap_extra_tdo_w[0]), which is the tie the DTP port table prescribes for an unused chain. Two enrolled tests read the loops: smu_dtp_bsr_extest_loopback_test (BSR_EXTEST_TDO_MATCH, the BSR loop; its plan entry declares STUB:DECLARED BSR_TB_SCAN_LOOPBACK with the same limits) and smu_dtp_scan_chain_boundary_test (the three iJTAG host loops behind its chain-length and SIB-readback compares, and the I/O STAP TDO-to-TDI return under its select checks); smu_dtp_bsr_ijtag_scan_test reads the .select gate only and compares no TDO bit.

1.9. prim_sync2 / prim_sync3 stand-ins

Table 11. <code>prim_sync2</code> / <code>prim_sync3</code> stand-ins

Claim accepted

Every synchroniser output the bench observes on Verilator is produced by a two- or three-stage flop pipe that starts at a defined 0 with no reset, not by the ocah_prim cells the product elaborates; the product cells are on no Verilator proof path. The claims downstream — power-good stability, primary and cold reset release, the boot-stall gate, i2c_enable into the LSIO mux — are accepted as proven over the stand-in’s behaviour.

Why the real path cannot be used

The product prim_sync2 / prim_sync3 start with X on the first stage and the wrapper port exposes no rst_ni to clear it, so on a four-state view the X persists into every consumer; the stand-ins add initial '0 on each stage and change nothing else about the pipe.

1.10. eFuse models in the product composition

Table 12. eFuse models in the product composition

Claim accepted

The OTP word the JTAG2AXI OTP leaves read and write, and the eFuse image the SEP lifecycle controller consumes, are answered by efuse_bank_model behind efuse_interface_shim inside the wrapper’s IP integration, not by eFuse macros. Command decode, series and single-op sequencing, error responses and the shadow-register path through the shim are DUT logic and are proven; the macro’s storage, sense timing and programming are not. On the leaves that run without +skip_fuse_sense, the sense sequence the SMC and SEP eFuse controllers run, and the fuse_sense_done and shadow image it earns, are answered by the same models from the +smc_efuse_hex and +sep_efuse_hex images, so the controllers' sequencing is proven against the model’s response, not a macro’s timing.

Why the real path cannot be used

The eFuse macro is a technology cell outside the open-source tree; the composition instantiates the model in its place for every simulator.

Scope

hw/top/smc_ip_integration.sv instantiates u_efuse_interface_shim (efuse_interface_shim) and u_efuse_bank_model (efuse_bank_model); hw/top/sep_ip_integration.sv instantiates the same pair for the SEP. Both are part of the DUT compile on every profile, so every wrapper leaf elaborates them; they are load-bearing for smu_dtp_otp_smc_map_rw_test, smu_dtp_otp_smc_complete_rw_test, smu_dtp_otp_smc_series_error_test, smu_otp_vs_fabric_map_race_test, smu_sep_efuse_test, and the sep_lifecycle and sep_chain leaves, and they answer the real sense on the eight wrapper leaves that carry no +skip_fuse_sense: the five boot-stall leaves (smu_boot_stall_jtag_cold_reset_matrix_test, smu_boot_stall_vs_ic_reset_priority_test, smu_clock_stop_coordination_test, smu_dft_dtp_boot_stall_test, smu_dft_gpio_boot_stall_test) and smu_sep_lcc_flow_test on sep_rtl, each above a Real fuse sense (no +skip_fuse_sense) reason line, and smu_sep_fuse_sense_done_test and smu_smc_fuse_sense_sequence_test on sep_rtl_fuse_sense. On those eight the smc_fuse_reset_n_delayed_o release, the SEP lc_state and dbg_disable posture and the sense-completion ordering they observe are the controllers' own sense of the model-held image.
