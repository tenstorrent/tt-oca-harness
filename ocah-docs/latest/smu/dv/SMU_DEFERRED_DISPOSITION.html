1. SMU deferred and OUT disposition

This chapter has two parts. The table below is the catalog of every SMU test name classified deferred or OUT, and none of those names is in the hosted credit set hosted; Bench stand-ins and exceptions is the record of every stand-in, force, skip and waiver on a proof path of an enrolled test. The table first. Three kinds of name are in it. Fifteen have a test body and are enrolled in testlists/all.toml; their bucket says which group holds them and why that group is not credit. Seven have a test body under hw/sys/smu/dv/cocotb_wrapper/tests/ or cocotb/tests/ and are enrolled in no testlist, because they cannot run or cannot pass on either bench; their bucket says what each needs. The rest have no test body in this tree. None of the last two kinds is reportable as PASS. Enrolled names outside hosted that carry neither classification — the lifecycle, debug-gating, chain and elaboration leaves — are not in this table: each is carded in SMU_VPLAN.adoc and enrolled in a named group of testlists/all.toml.

Buckets:

  • SEP1-enrolled — has a body and runs in the wrapper all group with real SEP instruction retirement (sep_real_fw, sep_entropy, or fw_smoke for the SEP smoke). Not hosted credit, and no CI workflow schedules those groups.

  • SEP1-blocked — has a body under cocotb_wrapper/tests/, enrolled in no testlist: the SEP-driven SMC bring-up polls SMC SRAM that no sys-inbound master can reach, so the leaf cannot pass by construction. Re-enrols in all the day the SMC SRAM path exists.

  • not-runnable — has a body, enrolled in no testlist: the claim it checks is not met by the RTL as built, or the bench cannot produce the condition it needs. The row names the condition.

  • OUT-until-SEP1 — no body; needs real SEP instruction retirement under SMU and SEP=1 TB ports.

  • child-TB-owned — protocol or CPU depth belongs to the SMC, SEP, or DTP TB.

  • waived — superseded, toggle-as-pass, stimulus-pin gap, or leftover catalog name.

Enrolled names outside this catalog:

  • The JTAG2AXI / OTP / filter set in hosted is recorded in SMU_HOSTED_COMPONENT_SIGNOFF.adoc, which also records ATOP reject as OUT.

  • smu_ext_boot_seq_gate_test and smu_smc_mailbox_int_test are enrolled in hosted (testlists/all.toml).

  • The one unenrolled body under cocotb/tests/, smu_ext_axi_global_addr_smoke_test, is in the table below under not-runnable.

OTP-vs-fabric concurrency on the MAP shadow word

waived

smu_otp_vs_fabric_map_race_test carries a race name it does not earn, and this row is the reason it keeps it. Both writers are JTAG2AXI bridges behind the one TAP, so their DR shifts are serialised by construction and the OTP op has completed before the fabric write can be issued; the enrolled test therefore claims back-to-back coherence, not overlap, and says so in its header and plan entry. Genuine overlap needs a second, independent master on the shadow-map word, which the wrapper’s product interfaces do not provide. Not renamed here because the name is carried by the testlist, the evidence map, the feature list and the plan anchor, and the rename would ride inside a branch already this large; it belongs in a standalone change.

smu_cold_reset_async_assert_test

not-runnable

Body kept under cocotb_wrapper/tests/; enrolled in no testlist. Holds every wrapper clock static and asserts rst_cold_ni, expecting the cold-stable and primary-reset outputs to fall without a clock edge (port_table.adoc: "Asynchronous assertion"); the SMC reset controller samples rst_cold_ni through a clk_ref_i deglitch shift register, so with the clocks stopped no output moves. Enrols when the specification or the RTL settles which is right; that decision needs a tracker issue naming port_table.adoc and smc_reset_ctrl.

smu_dtp_jtag2axi_abort_mid_op_test (wrapper)

not-runnable

The scenario is enrolled on --dut smu_block (testlists/block.toml, all). On the wrapper the real SMC eFuse model answers a SINGLE_OP in tens of clk_smu against a 39-bit DR shift, so the BUSY window the abort needs never exists; a wrapper enrolment needs a stallable eFuse hook in tb/tb_wrapper_top.sv.

1.1. Bench stand-ins and exceptions

A stand-in is anything that answers in place of the mechanism a claim is about: a testbench force, a simulation-only path selected by a plusarg, a configuration waiver, a loopback where a chain would be, a compiled-in substitute for a product cell, or a model inside the product composition. Seven sit on proof paths of enrolled SMU tests. Each has one entry of six fields. The first three — the claim that is accepted with the stand-in in place, why the real path cannot be used, and the exact scope — are filled from the tree. The last three — approver, approval date against a revision, and review or expiry date — are filled by a person; until they are, the entry is a declaration and clears nothing, and every test on its scope is graded as if the stand-in were undeclared. pending in those fields means exactly that. Each entry describes the tree at this branch’s head, not an older revision: the stand-ins and the entries that declare them move in the same commits, so a hash written into this file is stale the moment the file is edited. What makes an entry checkable instead is the command in Re-confirming a stand-in — one per entry, each naming a string a reader can see for themselves. An approver fills the Date, revision field with the revision they checked and the command they ran.

1.1.2. +esrc_noise_force

Claim accepted

The decorrelator, health tests, CSRNG and EDN run end to end on testbench-driven noise, and the SEP firmware brings that stack up and consumes its output. The ring-oscillator noise source itself is not proven: esrc_noise_active_o is the forced node and tracks the driven bit by construction, so the first checkpoint of smu_sep_entropy_test is a check of the force having taken, not of the source.

Why the real path cannot be used

The ESRC ring oscillators rely on #delay feedback, which Verilator ignores, so the twelve noise lanes never toggle and no entropy is produced. The SEP DV bench takes the same exception at the same node.

Scope

Only under +esrc_noise_force; only the twelve dcor.noise_i lanes (gen_ecmplx[0..11].u_generator.u_decorrelator.noise_i), re-forced every clk_smu_i edge from esrc_noise_ext_i, which SmuEsrcNoiseDriver drives with twelve 16-bit LFSRs from the fixed seed 0xACE1; every downstream tap is observe-only. The plusarg is carried by three testlists/all.toml entries: smu_sep_entropy_test (its verdict requires esrc_noise_took_o), smu_sep_modules_test (its entropy stage), and smu_sep_aes_test (a prerequisite outside its AES-128 known-answer claim). The SEP bench’s +sep_crypto_edn_force has no counterpart here.

1.1.3. +skip_fuse_sense with the shadow preload

Claim accepted

On the leaves that pass it, the SMC eFuse shadow registers — and on the sep_rtl profile the SEP ones — hold the image the preload file supplies, and the sense FSM that would have earned that image from the eFuse macro is skipped. The consumers of the sensed image are under test on those leaves: the lifecycle controller, lc_state at the SMC and dbg_disable at the DTP, the OTP word the JTAG2AXI leaves read, and the reset and boot state downstream of the fuse-reset release. The sense FSM is not. The five boot-stall leaves (smu_boot_stall_jtag_cold_reset_matrix_test, smu_boot_stall_vs_ic_reset_priority_test, smu_clock_stop_coordination_test S1/S2, smu_dft_dtp_boot_stall_test, smu_dft_gpio_boot_stall_test) are not on this scope: they run without the plusarg, so the smc_fuse_reset_n_delayed_o release they gate is the SMC eFuse controller’s own sense completion against the +smc_efuse_hex image, and their gate claim is judged on that run. smu_smc_reset_ctrl_test, smu_ext_boot_seq_gate_test and smu_smc_smoke_test read the reset and boot state downstream of the release the boot-stall leaves prove; the OTP leaves (smu_dtp_otp_smc_map_rw_test, smu_dtp_otp_smc_complete_rw_test, smu_dtp_otp_smc_series_error_test, smu_otp_vs_fabric_map_race_test) compare against a shadow word that was preloaded. The lifecycle leaves (sep_lifecycle, sep_chain) declare their +sep_shadow_reg_preload image as the stimulus, so for them the preload is setup, not a stand-in. smu_sep_lcc_flow_test is not on this scope either: it runs without the plusarg, the SEP shadow registers it observes are the SEP eFuse controller’s own sense of the +sep_efuse_hex image, and its sense claim is judged on that run.

Why the real path cannot be used

On the block bench tb/tb_top.sv ties the eFuse shim command and bank-controller responses to zero, so no sense can complete and primary reset would never release. On the wrapper the real path is available — hw/top/smc_ip_integration.sv instantiates efuse_interface_shim and efuse_bank_model, and the boot-stall leaves use it — so for the remaining wrapper leaves the skip is a run-time and stimulus convenience: the SMC sense of the 768-word image takes 2280-2330 clk_smu after primary reset release on Verilator, and the preloaded shadow image, not a sensed one, is the value the OTP and lifecycle leaves declare as their input.

Scope

testlists/all.toml: 81 of 96 entries carry +skip_fuse_sense and +smc_shadow_reg_preload=hw/sys/smc/dv/assets/default_efuse_shadow_reg.preload in their own args, each above a one-line reason; the sep_rtl entries that carry the skip, other than smu_sep_lcc_flow_test, add +sep_shadow_reg_preload=assets/default_sep_efuse_shadow_reg.preload, and the lifecycle leaves name their own image. The [run_modes.*] tables of smu_sim_cfg.toml carry no eFuse plusarg. smu_block_sim_cfg.toml [run_modes.smoke], [smc], [dtp], [fabric] still pass both, for the block-bench reason above. The plusarg is live only when the SIMULATION define is set (hw/common/dv/configs/profiles/native.toml sets it on every target); a build without it senses. tb/tb_wrapper_top.sv exports the SEP shadow registers' sim_skip_fuse_sense flag as sep_fuse_sense_skipped_o, and the SMC shadow registers print Skipping fuse sense or Not skipping fuse sense at time zero, so a log tells which path ran.

1.1.5. +define+ASSERTS_OFF

Claim accepted

No SystemVerilog assertion inside the DUT, the vendored common cells or the testbench is a checker on any run this package records. Every enrolled verdict is a Python compare in a sequence or scoreboard, or the firmware’s own terminal loop, and every card in SMU_VPLAN.adoc offers such a compare as its evidence; no card offers an SVA. What is not proven is whatever the compiled-out assertions would have caught: a protocol or ordering violation inside a block that no enrolled compare reads.

Why the real path cannot be used

The vendored pulp-platform/common_cells assertions are guarded by COMMON_CELLS_ASSERTS_OFF, which hw/common/dv/configs/profiles/native.toml sets for every DUT, and the OCAH ASSERTS_OFF guard is set on every Verilator target of both SMU sim configs so that the immediate assertions in the PeakRDL register blocks and the SEP blocks do not fire on the two-state, X-free elaboration for reasons unrelated to any enrolled claim. Turning them on needs a per-assertion review of which fire legitimately on Verilator, which is the shared shim and assertion-gating work of issue #1164.

Scope

smu_sim_cfg.toml and smu_block_sim_cfg.toml: +define+ASSERTS_OFF on every Verilator target; hw/common/dv/configs/profiles/native.toml: COMMON_CELLS_ASSERTS_OFF. One compile serves every leaf of a bench, so the scope is every leaf of both benches on Verilator; the recorded build_args of any run carry both defines. The VCS view of --dut smu_block --framework uvm compiles with the same defines.

1.1.6. Scan-chain loopbacks

Claim accepted

A bit shifted out of the DTP’s BSR host scan port returns on its scan input one TCK later, so smu_dtp_bsr_extest_loopback_test proves the DTP’s EXTEST decode, its scan-control gating and the IEEE 1149.1 TDO retimer against a zero-length testbench chain. Why the real path cannot be used

The wrapper has no boundary-scan cells and no adopter DFD or DFT scan chains; those host ports terminate at pads the open-source tree does not model.

1.1.7. prim_sync2 / prim_sync3 stand-ins

Claim accepted

Every synchroniser output the bench observes on Verilator is produced by a two- or three-stage flop pipe that starts at a defined 0 with no reset, not by the och_prim cells the product elaborates; the product cells are on no Verilator proof path. The claims downstream — power-good stability, primary and cold reset release, the boot-stall gate, i2c_enable into the LSIO mux — are accepted as proven over the stand-in’s behaviour.

Why the real path cannot be used

The product prim_sync2 / prim_sync3 start with X on the first stage and the wrapper port exposes no rst_ni to clear it, so on a four-state view the X persists into every consumer; the stand-ins add initial '0 on each stage and change nothing else about the pipe.

Scope

smu_sim_cfg.toml and smu_block_sim_cfg.toml [build].stubs list hw/sys/smc/dv/tb/verilator_stubs/prim_sync2.sv and prim_sync3.sv; the runner emits them ahead of the Bender filelist so Verilator’s first-definition-wins picks them, and drops them on VCS. 1.1.8. eFuse models in the product composition

Claim accepted

The OTP word the JTAG2AXI OTP leaves read and write, and the eFuse image the SEP lifecycle controller consumes, are answered by efuse_bank_model behind efuse_interface_shim inside the wrapper’s IP integration, not by eFuse macros.
