= Threat Model
:description: SEP threat categories, attacker assumptions, and limits of protection.

[[threat-model]]

The SEP threat model considers remote software attacks, physical attacks and
supply-chain attacks. These categories describe the threats considered;
the explicit limits of protection are listed below.

Software attacks apply across deployments and are the primary attack vector
for data center implementations. Physical attacks are localized and require
close proximity; they are relevant to automotive and robotics applications.
Supply-chain attacks involve changes to the design, production or provisioning
of chips.

[[sep-threat-model-software]]
== Software Attacks

* Malicious firmware modification during boot, overlay or application loading,
  or updates.
* Modification or exfiltration of private data, such as ML model weights and
  cryptographic keys.
* Forged attestation that mimics legitimate device behavior.

[[sep-threat-model-physical]]
== Physical Attacks

The model assumes that an attacker has full visibility of SRAM and OTP data
state.

Physical threats considered include:

* Fault injection to bypass security checks, including clock and voltage
  glitching, laser and electromagnetic (EM) energy injection, and thermal
  gradients. The model also groups logically induced faults, such as Row
  Hammer DRAM attacks, in this category.
* Differential cryptanalysis for key recovery, and side-channel attacks to
  extract keys or other secret data.
* Hardware probing to observe or control data within a chiplet or in
  communication between chiplets.

[[sep-threat-model-supply-chain]]
== Supply-Chain Attacks

* Insider modification of the design to disable or weaken security features
  or insert backdoors, and theft of hardware secrets such as security tokens.
* Modification of IC design masks to change behavior or insert trojans.
  For example, modified BL0 boot ROM contents could disable secure boot.
* Corruption of fuse provisioning to enable or disable features without
  authorization or leak device-unique secrets.

[[sep-threat-model-protection-limits]]
== Limits of Protection

SEP does not explicitly protect against the following threats, even though
some appear in the categories above:

* Hardware probing of communications between chiplets inside the package.
* Hardware probing of chiplets inside the package and certain physical
  attacks, such as bias fault injection.
* Thermal gradients used to influence the entropy source or induce timing
  faults.
* Supply-chain attacks.
